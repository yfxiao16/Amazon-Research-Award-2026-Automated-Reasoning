\section{Proposed Research}

The core of \sysname is a neurosymbolic loop, in which LLM agents propose decompositions (Thrust~2), a contract verification engine certifies them against a hierarchical contract network (Thrust~1), and the outcome of verification is returned to the agents to guide specification repair and learning (Thrust~3).

\thrust{Thrust 1: {Design Representations: Hierarchical Contract Networks.}} A complex design must be decomposed along the dimensions. Spatial decomposition splits a system into interacting components, as in our work on architectures~\cite{xiao2024contrarc}, distributed systems~\cite{xiao2026memocode}, and multi-robot systems~\cite{xiao2026acies}. Hierarchical decomposition refines requirements across abstraction levels that use different formalisms, which our prior work relates through contract embeddings based on conservative approximations~\cite{naik2024contract}{\cite{wang2022hierarchical}}, so that a refinement proved on the embedded contracts also holds for the original ones. Temporal decomposition splits a mission into contracts on consecutive time intervals, each enforced by a time-varying control barrier function~\cite{waqas2022cbf}. Existing results address each dimension in isolation. Thrust~1 will {develop representations of the design flow in terms of hierarchical contract networks}, in which components are related by composition, by refinement across formalisms, and by sequencing in time, together with conditions under which the correctness of each local step implies the correctness of the whole network. {The contracts themselves will be expressed using domain-specific formalisms. In a multi-robot system design, contracts will couple a Generalized Reactivity(1) (GR(1)) mission layer~\cite{bloem2012gr1} with controllers certified by control barrier functions~\cite{ames2019cbf} or reachability analysis, while learned policies, such as \gls{vla} models~\cite{kim2024openvla}, will be guarded at run time by monitors and safety filters. Hardware contracts will relate the ports of the \gls{rtl} modules over clock cycles. The components may also be LLM-based agents, to which subtasks may be assigned via contracts.}

Timing contracts for distributed systems associate the logical ordering of events with execution and communication latencies, and bound the inconsistency and unavailability of each component~\cite{xiao2026memocode}. Robot contracts couple a Generalized Reactivity(1) (GR(1)) mission layer~\cite{bloem2012gr1} with controllers certified by control barrier functions~\cite{ames2019cbf} or reachability analysis. Learned policies, such as \gls{vla} models~\cite{kim2024openvla}, are guarded at run time by safety filters and monitors that report contract violations to the decomposition, and are post-trained with rewards derived from their component contracts. Hardware contracts relate the ports of \gls{rtl} modules over clock cycles.

\thrust{Thrust 2: Agentic Decomposition Search Methods.} \sysname will represent a design as a hierarchy of contracts that LLM agents build recursively, starting from the system requirements. At each level, an agent proposes contracts for the subcomponents, guided by certified decompositions of similar components retrieved from a library of certified contracts{~\cite{iannopollo2014library,wang2022hierarchical}} (Thrust~3), and the engine accepts the proposal only if the subcomponents together are certified to meet the parent contract. Each accepted subcomponent is either verified directly by a back end or handed to a new agent that works only on its own contract. When a component cannot meet its contract, the parent agent revises the decomposition and keeps the certified parts whose contracts are unchanged. {The agents will explore alternatives by re-defining the component boundaries, reassigning responsibilities among components, or introducing intermediate contracts at another abstraction level. The engine will prune candidates that violate compatibility or refinement conditions.} Our methodology will be portable across domains, since only the contract formalisms and the associated back-end tools will need to be adapted. 

\thrust{Thrust 3: {Evolution via Feedback from Verification.}} Every failed verification step returns a diagnosis that points to the responsible portions of the contracts, e.g., a counterexample trace or a minimal set of conflicting requirements. The agents access the tools through a \gls{mcp} server. We will use these outcomes at three levels. First, diagnoses guide repair actions on the design specification or implementation, performed by formal methods~\cite{xiao2026memocode} or by the agents. Second, recurring failures are generalized into patterns that exclude entire families of candidate decompositions~\cite{xiao2024contrarc} and are stored, together with the certified contracts, in libraries that evolve with each design~\cite{xiao2026acies}. Third, the verdicts of the engine serve as certified rewards to post-train the proposing models and the design policies via reinforcement learning. In all cases, the correctness of an accepted design never depends on the proposing agents.

\thrust{Evaluation and preliminary results.} Our prior work improves the reliability of auto-formalization by screening diverse candidate formalizations with solvers~\cite{ni2026draftprune} and assesses reasoning agents with solver-backed checks~\cite{ni2026agentified}. For distributed systems, our timing contracts~\cite{xiao2026memocode} verified and repaired the consistency and availability requirements of an automatic emergency braking system. In multi-robot systems, ACIES~\cite{xiao2026acies} decomposes mission requirements into GR(1) contracts, synthesizes controllers, and executes them under control barrier functions, and our ongoing work extends it with runtime monitors and a library of certified components. In hardware, component contracts will be written as \glspl{sva} and proved by hardware model checkers on open-source RTL designs.

{Finally, we will consider a multi-agent LLM system as an additional case study.} We will compare frontier models with and without feedback from {verification} and retrieval against the post-trained proposer, including on faulty RTL designs, measuring the requirements certified beyond direct checking, the acceptance rate, solver runs and tokens per certificate, and library reuse.
