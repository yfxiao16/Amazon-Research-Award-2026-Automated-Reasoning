\textbf{Abstract -} \Gls{llm} agents are increasingly employed to design and verify complex systems, such as \gls{rtl} hardware, controllers for teams of robots, and workflows of cooperating software agents. Their design requires decomposing system-level requirements into component-level specifications, which is a hard problem for temporal specifications: distributed reactive synthesis is undecidable in general, and finding a decomposition whose components are realizable and whose composition is correct is still largely an art. In this project, we propose a framework, termed Agentic Contract-Based Multilevel Engineering (\sysname), for sound decomposition of hardware, robotic, and agentic software systems based on \gls{ag} contracts. Agents propose contracts at every level of a design hierarchy, and a contract engine with model checking and synthesis back ends decides whether each step is sound. \sysname follows a three-pronged approach: a theory of decomposition for temporal contracts, including sound approximations of the contract quotient and circular reasoning across components; methods to decompose agentic tasks into roles with contracts that provably refine the task, optimized jointly with the cost of executing the roles; and formal feedback that localizes failures, repairs decompositions, and reuses certified components across designs. \sysname will be built on \chase, our open-source contract-based design framework, and evaluated on hardware, multi-robot, and multi-agent software benchmarks.

\textbf{Keywords --} assume-guarantee contracts, compositional verification, reactive synthesis, GR(1), neurosymbolic reasoning, LLM agents, hardware formal verification, autoformalization.
