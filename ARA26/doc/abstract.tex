\textbf{Abstract -} \Gls{llm} agents are increasingly employed to design and verify complex systems, such as \gls{rtl} hardware, controllers for teams of robots, and workflows of cooperating software agents. Their design and verification require decomposing system-level requirements into component-level specifications. Decomposing temporal specifications is a hard problem: distributed reactive synthesis is undecidable in general, and finding a decomposition whose components are realizable and whose composition is correct is still largely an art. This project aims to develop the theory and the computational tools for sound agentic decomposition based on \gls{ag} contracts. The proposed framework, termed Agentic Contract-Based Multilevel Engineering (\sysname), uses agents as heuristics that propose contracts at every level of a design hierarchy, while the soundness of each step is decided by a contract engine together with model checking and reactive synthesis back ends. We will address three open problems: the decomposition of GR(1) contracts into realizable component contracts, including sound approximations of the contract quotient within GR(1) and circular reasoning across components; the preservation of temporal obligations when long-horizon agents compact their context or delegate tasks to sub-agents; and the use of counterexamples, unrealizable cores, and certificates as formal feedback for repair and reuse. \sysname will be built on \chase, our open-source contract-based design framework, and evaluated on hardware, multi-robot, and multi-agent software benchmarks.

\textbf{Keywords --} assume-guarantee contracts, compositional verification, reactive synthesis, GR(1), neurosymbolic reasoning, LLM agents, hardware formal verification, autoformalization.
