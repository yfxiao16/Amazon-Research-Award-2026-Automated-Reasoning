\textbf{Abstract -} \Gls{llm} agents are increasingly employed to design complex systems, such as distributed cyber-physical systems, multi-robot systems, and hardware. However, AI-generated designs often contain errors: agents split a design into parts that do not fit together, drift from their instructions over long design tasks, and leave no record that allows an error to be traced back to its cause. In this project, we propose a framework, termed \sysname, for sound and traceable decomposition of complex systems based on \gls{ag} contracts. In \sysname, agents propose a contract for each component at every level of a design hierarchy, and a contract engine accepts a step only when it certifies that the components together meet their parent's requirements. \sysname follows a three-pronged approach. First, it extends contract theory so that every decomposition step, even across formalisms and abstraction levels, can be checked by existing verification and synthesis tools. Second, it develops an agentic decomposition search guided by a library of certified components, in which only the leaf verifiers change across domains. Finally, it lets the agents learn from formal feedback, where counterexamples repair decompositions and serve as verifiable rewards to post-train the proposing models. \sysname will extend \chase, our open-source contract-based design framework, with LLM agents and will be evaluated on distributed systems, multi-robot systems, and hardware.

\textbf{Keywords --} assume-guarantee contracts, compositional verification, distributed systems, GR(1), neurosymbolic reasoning, LLM agents, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, hardware formal verification.
