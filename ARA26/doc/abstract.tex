\textbf{Abstract -} \Gls{llm} agents are increasingly employed to design complex systems, such as \gls{rtl} hardware and controllers for teams of robots. However, AI-generated designs are error-prone: agents decompose problems incorrectly, drift from their instructions over long design tasks, and leave no record that allows an error to be traced back to its cause. Decomposition is also hard in itself, as distributed reactive synthesis is undecidable in general. In this project, we propose a framework, termed Agentic Contract-Based Multilevel Engineering (\sysname), for sound and traceable decomposition of complex systems based on \gls{ag} contracts. In \sysname, agents propose contracts at every level of a design hierarchy, and a contract engine with model checking and synthesis back ends certifies each step. \sysname follows a three-pronged approach: an extension of contract theory with decidable classes of decomposition, sound approximations of the contract quotient, and circular reasoning; an agentic decomposition search in which LLM agents, guided by a library of certified components, propose contracts and only the leaf verifiers change across domains; and learning from formal feedback, in which counterexamples and unrealizable cores repair decompositions in context and serve as verifiable rewards to post-train the models that propose them. \sysname will be built on \chase, our open-source contract-based design framework, and evaluated primarily on hardware designs and on multi-robot missions.

\textbf{Keywords --} assume-guarantee contracts, compositional verification, reactive synthesis, GR(1), neurosymbolic reasoning, LLM agents, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, hardware formal verification, autoformalization.
