\textbf{Abstract --} \Gls{llm} agents are increasingly employed to tackle the design of complex engineered systems, such as distributed cyber-physical systems, multi-robot systems, and integrated circuits. However, existing approaches lead to outcomes that are often unsatisfactory. Agents may propose to decompose a design into parts that are not fully compatible, tend to drift from the specifications over long horizon tasks, and leave no record that allows an error to be traced back to its root cause. In this project, we propose \sysname, a framework for assured and traceable design of complex systems via decomposition  based on \gls{ag} contracts. In \sysname, the agents propose a decomposition of a complex design task into  contracts at each level of a design hierarchy, and a contract verification engine accepts a step only when it certifies that the lower-level contracts are implementable, and together refine the higher-level requirements. We will develop \sysname by following a three-pronged approach. First, we will investigate representations of the design in terms of hierarchical contract networks that combine decomposition in space, over time, and across abstraction levels, and link every step of the design flow to verification and synthesis tools. Second, we will develop an agentic decomposition search method guided by a library of certified contracts. The methods applies to multiple domains, and can be transferred to a new domain by instantiating the appropriate  formalism, to express the contracts, and the corresponding algorithms and tools to reason about them. Finally, we will develop mechanisms enabling the agents to learn from the outcome of verification, where diagnoses such as counterexamples guide repair actions on the design specification or implementation, and verdicts serve as certified rewards to post-train the proposing models or design policies. \sysname will extend \chase, our open-source contract-based design framework, with LLM agents and will be evaluated in two application domains: integrated circuit design and warehouse multi-robot systems.

\textbf{Keywords --} Assume-guarantee contracts, compositional verification, distributed systems, GR(1), neurosymbolic reasoning, LLM agents, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, hardware formal verification.
