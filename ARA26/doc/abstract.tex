\textbf{Abstract --} \Gls{llm} agents are increasingly employed to design complex systems, such as distributed cyber-physical systems, multi-robot systems, and hardware. However, AI-generated designs often contain errors: agents split a design into parts that do not fit together, drift from their instructions over long design tasks, and leave no record that allows an error to be traced back to its cause. In this project, we propose a framework, termed \sysname, for sound and traceable decomposition of complex systems based on \gls{ag} contracts. In \sysname, agents propose a contract for each component at every level of a design hierarchy, and a contract engine accepts a step only when it certifies that the components together meet their parent's requirements. \sysname follows a three-pronged approach. First, it develops a theory of contract networks that combines decomposition in space, across abstraction levels, and over time, so that every step can be checked by existing verification and synthesis tools. Second, it develops an agentic decomposition search guided by a library of certified contracts. The search applies to a new domain by changing only the contract formalism and the back-end verification tools. Finally, it lets the agents learn from formal feedback, where diagnoses such as counterexamples guide repair and solver verdicts serve as verifiable rewards to post-train the proposing models and robot policies. \sysname will extend \chase, our open-source contract-based design framework, with LLM agents and will be evaluated in all three domains.

\textbf{Keywords --} assume-guarantee contracts, compositional verification, distributed systems, GR(1), neurosymbolic reasoning, LLM agents, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, hardware formal verification.
